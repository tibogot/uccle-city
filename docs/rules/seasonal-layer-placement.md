**Rule.** A seasonal / event layer stands only where the city can hold it: every placed object on paving (never a roadway, tram track or parking strip), clear of tram tracks, walls (by its full reach: a fir's branches), tree trunks and shop doors; anything hung over a street clears traffic (>= 4.4 m) and the tram's contact wire; lights are emissive (rule 7). Foliage seen from the street (the fir) is NEEDLES, not slabs: its near level is alpha-TESTED needle cards (never blended) of modelled sprays, shaded with CANOPY normals (never the card's flat normal) on both faces, the far levels solid and cheap; a triangle budget per level. Round 3: the near level's cards are drawn as a depth PRE-PASS twin (alpha + band, no colour) + a lit pass with depth EQUAL and no discard (the lit shader once per visible pixel); alpha-tested cards never cast into the sun's map (the FAR level's solid fronds cast the shadow of every level: the same shadow on both sides of each switch); a level switch is invisible: the next level keeps the look (the middle level is big cards of the SAME atlas and material round a thin dark core), its match checked from the street at the game's pixel density (silhouette IoU, brightness, colour, see-through) and its cost by an offline model re-run for each kit (<= 0.1 ms at 8 m, then measured in the browser).

**Why.** the first Saint-Job fir stood 2.9 m from the square's pavilion (its branches went into it) and two market chalets touched it; the shop garlands hung behind the cornices (browser turns 1-2, claude/christmas); the fir's foliage was closed lumpy sleeves with a stroke pattern, lit by their own surface: up close it read as flat leaves / cards, not a fir (claude/xmasfir); the needle cards cost +0.26 ms at 8 m (budget 0.1): the lit alpha-tested shader ran on ~6.6 card layers per visible pixel and the cards' alpha-tested shadow was redrawn into the sun's cache; the middle level (solid fronds) read as flat tiers next to the needle crown at 45 m (claude/xmasfir3)
