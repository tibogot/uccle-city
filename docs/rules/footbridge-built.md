**Rule.** Every foot bridge over the railway (UrbIS Land cover "Bridges" PB, OSM footway bridge=yes) is a built, walkable structure, never a slab or a painted path: its deck spans the tracks at its measured level (DSM / the streets at its ends), clear of the catenary, its stairs or ramps go down to the platforms and the streets the OSM steps / footways lead to, railings on every side over a drop, and every end MEETS what is drawn there (the street, the path, the platform top) within 0.15 m: the terrain is carved off the treads, the walls go down into the ground, a welded end keeps the street it meets. Its OSM footways and steps are then not painted on the ground (the stripe would run down the banks and across the ballast). A footbridge is data (data/rail/footbridges.json: straight parts a -> b, width, levels as numbers or "ground") built by one procedural module (viewer/rail/footbridge.js) in the tile worker, one mesh per tile in the decks' materials, walked via world.decks and the third person's BVH; `?footbridge=old` turns it off.

**Why.** The two Vivier d'Oie station footbridges (PB 751 / 752, owner defect list 2026-11-17) were missing. railData.js drew every UrbIS bridge polygon over the line as a flat slab at the DTM of its centroid (bare earth: the cutting floor, in the ballast); vivier (2026-11-17) removed every slab left in a cutting, and nothing built what they stood for: bridgeAudit U3 listed both as "allowed" (known open), the land cover still painted OSM 36870319 / 209187984 and their steps as gravel stripes down one bank, over the tracks and up the other. No check asked whether a foot bridge was walkable or where its stairs landed. The first build also showed what a footbridge needs that a road deck does not: the bank covered the stairs cut into it (F4), and carving under a stair right to its end left a 0.16-0.25 m dip where it met the street (F2: the 1 m terrain grid cannot follow a stair's end; the stairs now end in a 1 m pad and the terrain within 1.4 m of a welded end only comes down to the walk).
