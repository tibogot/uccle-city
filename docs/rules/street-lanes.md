**Rule.** On every road the street model covers (a segment with at least one measured station: M / D driving ends, E where UrbIS maps nothing and the drawn road is the engine's own strip, X with measured ends), the traffic lanes ARE the model's lanes: offset curves of the segment's axis (the mid-line of the measured carriageway) at the model's driving-lane centres, smoothed along the whole segment (a running median and two box passes, C1), then held >= 1.0 m inside the measured driving ends AND the measured kerb / street-edge lines, and 1.13 m clear of the parked cars (a car's half width + 0.15; a wider vehicle moves out round a parked car in the sim), by ONE smooth offset profile (ramps <= 1:10, gaps under 30 m held: never point by point); on an X station one lane per direction, centred in its half of the measured carriageway; a two-way carriageway narrower than 3.2 m (a one-way one narrower than two clearances) carries one centred lane (the lanes moving apart continuously over the next metre of width, never a switch; a narrow carriageway's lane is held only within 0.3 m of its middle, not pinned to each station's measured middle) (the sim's narrow-stretch rule lets one direction through at a time). A segment end at a junction ends at the arm's MEASURED mouth (an unmeasured arm at the engine's trim, never further out); a degree-2 node inside a segment is one continuous curve. A junction turn between two model lanes is a quintic Hermite curve matching both lanes' end tangents and curvatures (G2), its handle chosen to keep the car body out of buildings and street-tree trunks, on the drawn carriageway and inside the junction polygon / floor disc, with the lowest peak curvature; where every handle still meets a building, a trunk or the pavement, the two lanes stop 1 m earlier, again, up to 8 m (each keeping max(3 m, 40 %)). Every junction turn of the final graph (an old-fit one too) that meets a building / trunk / pavement is laid again as the G2 turn where that does better. A segment half of a dual carriageway drawn as one OSM way (the OSM line beyond the measured carriageway's half width + 1.5 m, both directions driven) keeps the old fit. The tree clearing along the driven paths (viewer/streets/roadTrees.js) reads each trunk's own collider radius. In the sim, a car never waits inside a junction turn: it enters only when the lane past it holds it before that lane's own hold point or the next junction lets it through (don't block the box), and inside a turn it clears the junction before yielding to an ambulance. drivecheck walks the driven lanes / turns and, on model-covered roads, the model's axis over the roadway / parking (not the raw OSM centre line). viewer/traffic/tileTraffic.js stays the ONE builder for the runtime and every audit; laneShifter, the fitToSurface kerb push / parked-car shift / far snap and roadFollowTurns apply only to roads the model does not cover, and everywhere with `?streetlanes=old`. Every exception to the invariants is a gap-register entry by named cause.

**Why.** The lanes were the engine's OSM-swept lanes pushed sideways station by station (laneShifter after the line fit, the class raster push, the parked-car shifts, a far-lane snap through buildings, turns re-laid along far roads): each push was a local fix and the sum wove (slalom 7.9 km city-wide), cut junction corners and drove through houses where the OSM line was far off the street. The model already holds the measured carriageway and the lane placement the paint is drawn from; lanes laid from it once, as a road system lays them (an offset curve, G2 turns inside the junction), need no correction afterwards (claude/street4, docs/STREET_MODEL_PLAN.md job 4).

**Data cases.** Where a UrbIS building footprint overhangs a measured alley (I6 Venelle de Saint-Job: the façade's upper floors over the 7 m roadway), the lanes keep to the model's lanes on the measured carriageway, centred where it is narrow; they do not steer round the roof outline, which the 2 m roof grid cannot resolve. If such a lane is judged inside a building, the finding is a gap-register entry with cause `source-data` (the footprint is the roof's outline, not the ground floor's), never a lane bent round it.

**The sim (viewer/traffic/trafficSim.js).** A vehicle wider than a car passes a parked car by its own body: it tests its own corridor first (the lane keeps a car's clearance), may move out by its extra half width, searches the least clear shift (the lane's estimated need is a bounding disc), and only an oncoming vehicle within its real reach holds it; after 8 s refused, only the near oncoming traffic still does. A car standing on a conflicting turn with its front well short of the conflict point does not hold a crossing vehicle. A car that has moved on 3 m since its wait began is in a queue, not stuck. Inside a junction turn a car clears the junction before yielding to an ambulance.
