**Rule.** A gate row that asserts a wall time is a `timing: true` row and measures through `tools/gate/timing.mjs`: its budgets are multiplied by the calibrated slow-down `k` (a fixed loop timed before and after in the same process, the run discarded and measured again while the two differ by more than 15 %), a worst-step check takes each step's best over 3-5 repeats (or the best of the repeats), and it never times a window under 1 s with `process.cpuUsage()` (15.6 ms ticks on Windows). A COST RATIO between two variants of the same code (with / without a feature) is measured in this process's CPU time, not wall time, on the same work every time: `cpuAB()` runs rounds of A B B A blocks of a fixed amount of work, sums each runner's CPU time over its blocks and over reps on freshly built runners (the 15.6 ms tick averages out), and divides the variants' costs; runners meant to be identical must BE identical (no state shared between them, e.g. one clock that every runner advances). The gate runs the timing rows alone, first, one at a time.

**Why.** 4 of 13 logged gate runs failed on timing alone: stutter2 and mdrawcull asserted the max of one wall-time sample, which one preemption by another process (a census, another branch's gate) pushes over the budget; on 2026-11-20 with four gates on the laptop a garden slice measured 11-59 ms of wall time for 3.8 ms of work, and the sort-cost difference of two 300-frame bests swung 10-35 us (claude/speed2).

**Why the CPU-time ratio (claude/parking1, 2026-11-22).** The trafmix "sim cost" row (the step with the traffic mix <= 1.10 x without it) timed 30 interleaved rounds of 150 steps in wall time. The same code read x1.04-1.17 depending on what else ran on the laptop (claude/foundation failed it at x1.138 with two jobs beside it, and passed alone): the time another process held the core was counted as the mix's. CPU time of the stepping process is not; but CPU time alone still moved x1.05-1.12 run to run. The bigger cause was in the gate: the signal clock was ONE global that every sim's step advanced, so the two "identical" plain sims, stepped in turn, saw different signal phases and different traffic (0.87-1.21 x apart), and the ratio moved with them. With a clock per sim, CPU time summed over 4 reps of the same 30 rounds: x1.073 / 1.064 / 1.073 alone, the twin pair 0.998-1.034, while the CPU per unit of work itself varied 2 x with the load; with the all-tiles servicepass row (3 processes) running beside it, x1.084 / 1.049 / 1.060, then 1.057 alone.

**Why the median of three (coordinator, 2026-11-23).** Even in CPU time, one ratio still moved with the laptop's load: claude/lanes5 read x1.177 inside its gate while other agents' jobs (Blender, browser checks, other gates) ran, then x1.003 / 1.107 / 1.045 alone. One read over the limit is not a verdict. **Rule addition:** a cost-ratio check takes one read; if that read is over its limit, it takes two more and judges the MEDIAN of the three (trafmix's sim cost does this and prints all three reads). A real regression is over the limit in at least two of three reads and still fails.

**Why only clean reads count (coordinator, 2026-11-23, later the same day).** The median of three still failed claude/allocrate's merge (x1.116 / 1.030 / 1.139) while it passed alone (x1.046): another agent kept the laptop at ~40 % CPU, and the twin pair (two identical plain sims, timed the same way) swung x0.92-1.125. The ratio swung with it. The twin pair is the measurement's own noise meter. **Rule addition:** a cost-ratio read counts only if its twin is within 0.04 of 1. Up to 6 reads are taken until 3 count (a first read that is clean and under the limit is enough), and the median of the counted reads is judged. Too few clean reads is a FAIL that says "TOO NOISY TO MEASURE: rerun on a quiet machine", never a fake regression; every read is printed with its twin.
