**Rule.** A service vehicle (an ambulance or fire engine on a run, a bus, a bin lorry) always has a physical way. (1) Parking: every parked car leaves SERVICE_CLEAR = 3.0 m of carriageway across from its inner flank, to the far edge of its own carriageway (a kerb, a median, a tram reservation, an unsurveyed cell) or to the next parked car across (Brussels practice for the fire brigade; the widest service vehicle, a bus 2.55 m, + 2 x 0.2 m). Where both kerbs cannot keep it, the way's one-kerb side keeps its cars (the OSM parking tags, else the side with fewer driveways / stops, as the width rule picks it) and the other's go; where one side cannot, none (viewer/streets/parkPlace.js serviceClear; ?parkservice=old). No parked car within MOUTH_CLEAR = 5 m of a junction turn's end, a lane mouth (Belgian highway code art. 24; viewer/traffic/trafficGraph.js; ?parkmouth=old). (2) Routing: every lane has its clear width (trafficGraph.js laneClearWidths: the open width across the lane point over the drawn carriageway and past the parked boxes, a narrowing held over two stations 3 m apart); the router gives a service vehicle a lane under its width + 0.4 m only when there is no other way on (trafficSim.js clearWeight, x 0.001; ?clearroute=old). (3) The sim's courtesy, as drivers give it: a car keeping right for a run whose shifted body would run into a parked car ahead keeps its lane instead (?yieldkeep=old); at a narrow stretch (the two directions' lanes within 2.3 m) a car does not enter while a run comes the other way, and the run squeezes past oncoming cars that stand there when its body clears each by 0.15 m, at 2.5 m/s at most (?runsqueeze=old). (4) The trafmix gate gives the sim the parked cars main.js draws: a tile's cars under a loaded neighbour's core lane are dropped (viewer/traffic/borderPark.js laneHits).

**Why.** The PARKING job's two trafmix.stuckOld places (?streetlanes=old) were not what their name said. Dieweg E6 (seed 7, -962, 798): one kerb parked, 5.5 m clear; the oncoming cars kept right for the ambulance, pushed their bodies toward a parked car ahead, stopped for it for good; the ambulance waited at the narrow stretch for them to leave it: a deadlock in the yield, not the width. Rue Engeland D7 (seed 23, -1102, 1066): a bus queued behind a car waiting at a parked car 1.9 m from a lane's end, inside the posed turn's sweep, at a dual carriageway's merged junction whose mouths lie 13 m out from the node (the width rule keeps 12 m off a way's end only); the car was also under a neighbour's core lane, which the city never draws but the gate kept as an obstacle. With those fixed, the reshuffled seeds exposed two more run deadlocks of the same kind (an ambulance at a 100 m narrow street, 4.75 m clear, D6 (-1151, 837), cars entering it toward the run while the queue there stood at a red light) and a lane-mouth gridlock at D6 (-1254, 805) with parked cars inside the mouth. The kerb-to-kerb width alone also cannot see a street split by a tram reservation (Rue Engeland: 21 m kerb to kerb, 3.5-5 m each way), so the clearance is measured from each car across its own carriageway.
