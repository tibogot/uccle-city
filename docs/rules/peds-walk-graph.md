**Rule.** The people walk a graph built from what the city DRAWS, in the tile worker (viewer/pedestrians/walkGraph.js): the drawn sidewalks are the UrbIS class raster's sidewalk / ramp / path cells (the surfaces the city draws), thinned to their medial axis; squares a lattice over the square class; OSM paths only off the sidewalks; tram platforms from the tram=yes platform lines. Every walking line stays off the carriageway and out of every solid the drive colliders know (walls at body height, buildings, raised islands, hedges as walls); trunks and bollards are passed beside, never through. What only the main thread knows (benches, bins, poles, signs, lamps, parked cars) is cleared there in slices (viewer/pedestrians/cityGraph.js): an edge is bent round it, or closed. A new walking source (a footbridge's deck, a plaza) enters through the raster or a data layer the city draws, never as hand-placed lines. Neither the line geometry nor the people may be guessed from the OSM centre line or a fixed inset.

**Why.** Mr.doob's crowd walks fixed insets from his grid's street edges (his pedestrian-routes.js). Uccle's sidewalks are 1-6 m wide, cut by tree pits, lamp posts and bollards, and they wrap round junctions the street model draws as large polygons (Place de Saint-Job is one). The first attempt marched outward from the street model's kerbs and left out every sidewalk inside a junction polygon: Saint-Job's corners, about a third of its sidewalks. The medial axis of the drawn raster covers 97-99 % of the drawn sidewalk cells and joins the corners by itself. The walked length on a carriageway cell is 0.003-0.12 % (the raster's kerb cells). Every crossing (W3) and every person (S2, `pedssim`) is checked against the same raster.
