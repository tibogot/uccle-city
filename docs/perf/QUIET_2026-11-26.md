# Quiet-machine measures, 2026-11-26 (claude/perf5)

The owner was away and the laptop was quiet: this was the only job running. RTX 4050 laptop (enforced power limit 33.9 W),
16 GB, Chrome with WebGPU, one tab at 1280 x 720 x 1, in front, sound off (init script `uccle-sound=off`). The servers
were `tools/tour/serve.mjs` (perf5 on 8837 with a fresh worker bundle, villa3 on 8838; peds2 on 8840 and falconfun on
8839 were served by their own jobs).

## Machine state

- **System preflight** (tools/perf/preflight.ps1): YELLOW. The only WARN was the other jobs' idle city servers
  (8839, 8840, 8842: no tab on them). Other readings: mains, High performance, CPU 173 % of base, 8 % busy, 6.7 GB free,
  Chrome 0.8 GB, GPU 48 C and idle.
- **Page preflight** (debug/preflight.js, reference URL, 1280 x 720): CPU bench 67.9-84.2 ms vs 73.8 in the baseline.
  The fixed view's GPU time rose from 4.59 to 4.92, 5.57, 5.64 and 5.83 ms (baseline 4.52) over ten minutes as the GPU
  warmed up: 72-76 C, power-capped, clocks 2.4 -> 2.1-2.2 GHz. That gives YELLOW, then RED on the GPU line. It is the
  laptop's steady warm state, not another load. So every comparison below is an **alternated A/B**, and absolute GPU
  numbers carry about +-0.5 ms.
- **Harness finding: reload in a FRESH tab.** About 20 reloads in one tab filled the GPU's memory: 5.66 of 6.14 GB
  used, Chrome 5.5 GB, 3.2 GB free RAM. The next police drive then hit 0.6-6.9 s stalls (17 fps). Closing the tab freed
  it all (320 MB). Every police A/B run below used a new tab, with the old one closed. The villa, peds, falcon and
  season A/Bs ran earlier in one reused tab (alternated, so both sides shared the drift).

### Where the time goes

- **Per frame** (debug/renderCount.js, 572 frames of driving): 31.8 renders, 324 objects and 7.1 computes a frame.
  - The main scene pass costs **7.26 ms of CPU for 257 objects: ~28 us an object.**
  - The sun shadow movers cost 0.61 ms (30 objects).
  - A full sun-cache redraw costs 2.1 ms (108 casters) and hits 4.7 % of frames.
  - The SSR blurs cost 0.16 ms, the output 0.15, the grass compute 0.13. The other ~25 one-quad passes cost < 0.1 ms each.
- **Chrome trace** of 10 s of driving (the trace's own overhead included; tools/perf/trace_cpu.py plus a scratch
  per-stack aggregator): the main thread was busy 91 %.
  - The frame loop took 750 ms/s. Of that, the render took 566 ms/s and the updaters 127 ms/s (~2.2 ms a frame, ~90
    updaters, none over 0.45 ms).
  - Inside three's render: `_renderObjects` 438 ms/s, `updateMatrixWorld` 44 (2552 objects, 2422 auto-updating, 893
    visible), transparents 40, `projectObject` 22.
  - Per object (`_renderObjectDirect` 454 ms/s): Bindings.updateForRender 137 (uniform `writeBuffer` 29 of it),
    NodeManager.updateBefore 87 (the shadow pass's own render 50 inside it), draw 54, RenderObjects.get through
    roSweep's stamp 46 (9 of it the stamp itself), NodeManager.updateForRender 38, Geometries.updateForRender 37.
  - GC: 29 minor GCs in 18 s (67 ms in all, the longest 5 ms) and one major (34 ms).
  - The ForcedReflow insight: 0.3 ms (devHud), nothing to fix.
- **Slow frames by cause** (debug/perf5Frames.js, 45 s):
  - 147-225 frames ran over 16 ms of CPU.
  - A sun-cache full redraw came 167-194 times (6.5-7 % of frames), at 13.7-15.4 ms CPU vs 11.5-12.7 without one.
    In one sample, 154 of 242 redraws were the fixed 30-frame "refresh".
  - A GC frame came 95-105 times, at 12.6-15.0 ms CPU.
  - A tile event came 10-14 times, at 13.3-17.9 ms.
- **The worst frames are main-thread node builds**, the first draw of a material made from fresh nodes. In every drive
  from the station:
  - **The Chenaie timber playground's three fading pieces** (viewer/parkKit.js fadeCopy) came in at ~36-44 s: 10-19
    builds, 84-112 ms in one cluster, a **117-164 ms frame**.
  - **At each tile landing** (2 a drive, ~6.7 s and ~11.1 s), that tile's own asphalt graph took 27-32 ms and its terrain
    18-26 ms: 52-66 ms frames.
  - The walls' fading copy in the shadow pass took 29-37 ms at some landings.
  - The rail materials' first build took 40-61 ms at ~7.1 s (shared materials: once a session, not per tile).
- **Streaming**: the route crosses tile borders. G3, G4 and G5 loaded at 4-11 s, were dropped at 22.7 s, and G4 loaded
  again at 49 s. The landings are cheap now (main thread 39-94 ms in slices), but every pass makes new garbage.
- **GC**: one drive had a 695 MB heap drop at 8.3 s, an 83 ms frame. JS heap 1.2-1.75 GB.

### The top 5 police costs (ranked by how often they cost a frame)

1. **The steady CPU: the main pass's render submission.** ~7.3 ms of the 12-14 ms loop, for 257-277 render objects at
   ~28 us each (bindings / uniform uploads / draw). It leaves a p90 of 14-15 ms, so any extra work misses the vsync:
   56-93 frames over 25 ms in 45 s. GPU 5.7-7.7 ms: **the game is CPU-bound, not GPU-bound.**
2. **Main-thread node builds of per-tile / per-mesh graphs.** These are the worst frames: 117-164 ms at the playground,

4. **The sun shadow cache's full redraws.** 6.5-7 % of frames at +2-3 ms CPU. The fixed 30-frame refresh is ~2/3 of

5. **GC.** ~100 minor GCs a drive, at +1-2 ms on those frames, plus the occasional major one: a 695 MB drop and an

## Part 2: the fixes (claude/perf5, rule docs/rules/shared-node-graph.md, gate row `sharedgraph`)

- **Why.** three r187 keys its main-thread node builds by node ids. A material made from fresh nodes per mesh or per tile
  builds the same shader again on its first draw, inside that frame.
  - viewer/parkKit.js fadeCopy bound each fading mesh's fade value with `instancedBufferAttribute(attr)`: a graph per
    mesh.
  - viewer/roadPaint.js asphaltMaterial put each tile's marking atlas texture and its two uniforms into the graph: a
    graph per tile.
- **Fix 1, `?kitfadeshare=old` = before.** One fading copy per slot material, with one mask node. The fade value is the
  geometry attribute `kitFade`: each fading mesh gets a geometry that shares the piece's buffers and adds its own fade
  buffer.
- **Fix 2, `?asphaltshare=old` = before.** One template graph per atlas kind (none / R8 / RG). Each tile's material is a
  clone of it (the same node objects), and it reads its own atlas texture, scale and range per object from
  `material.userData`, through object-update nodes (lodFade's objectFade pattern).
- **Check.** tools/audit/sharedGraphCheck.mjs, gate row `sharedgraph`:
  - the kit's fading meshes share one material, one mask and one program key, each with its own buffer on a geometry
    that shares the piece's buffers; `update()` writes the values there; `setSlot` keeps the copy shared;
  - two tiles' asphalt materials share one graph and one program key, each with its own texture, scale and range, and
    the object-update nodes return the drawn material's values;
  - the old paths, run in a child process, still give a graph per mesh / tile;
  - the features rows exist.
- **Look.**
  - Asphalt, a 40 m view over Place Saint-Job's junction, old vs new: 0.063 % of pixels differ by > 20 (TAA noise).
  - The playground at the cross-fade distance (19 m, the tower drawn by both fading meshes): whole, no hole.

- The steady CPU and the 25-50 ms frames are unchanged: they are cost 1 (render submission) and costs 3-5.

## Next (ranked by the data)

1. **The same rule on the next two graphs.**
   - Each tile's terrain (`ground:textured`): 18-26 ms a landing. Its cover / ortho textures and bounds uniforms go to
     material userData through object-update nodes, the same pattern as the asphalt.
   - The walls' fading copy in the shadow pass: 29-37 ms.
   - Prewarm the rail materials under the loader: 40-61 ms, once.
2. **Steady CPU, fewer render objects** (cost 1). The main pass draws 257-277 objects:
   - props: 47, three LODs a kind;
   - tile groups: 70;
   - gardens: 24;
   - far-LOD tiles: 22.
   Merging each pool's LODs into one multi-draw, or marking static tile subtrees `matrixAutoUpdate = false`
   (`updateMatrixWorld` 0.75 ms), is the lever. Every ms off the 12 ms loop removes misses directly.
3. **The sun cache's 30-frame refresh** is 2/3 of its full redraws. Redraw on the events it covers (LOD hand-overs, pool
   changes), or spread it out; it needs a look check (tree wind shadows).
4. **Time to play:**
   - turn `?flushsplit=1` on, or cut the police flush's 8-10 s single task;
   - the falcon's 1.4-3.6 s "grass field" / "show" slices on the first tiles.
5. **Harness:** always use a fresh tab per page load in perf runs (rule one-chrome-tab: open the new tab, then close the
   old one). The GPU's memory fills across reloads in one tab.
